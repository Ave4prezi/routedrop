# RouteDrop founding-provider agreement — ATTORNEY REVIEW DRAFT

**NOT APPROVED, NOT FOR SIGNATURE, NOT A LEGALLY BINDING AGREEMENT.** This document identifies policy terms for independent legal review before real provider onboarding. The exact operating entity, terms effective date, governing law, consumer notices, cancellation requirements, insurance requirements and dispute remedies remain unapproved.

## 1. Parties and eligibility
- Identify the operating legal entity, address and authorized contracting party.
- Provider confirms authority to act for their business and accuracy of registration information.
- Provider offers only services for which it is qualified, legally authorized and adequately insured. Applicable categories and credential requirements must be reviewed by counsel.
- Platform may withhold listing eligibility pending independent verification. **Do not equate a user-uploaded document with verified status.**

## 2. Marketplace role and booking
- RouteDrop is intended to connect local customers with businesses offering available appointments, subject to review of applicable laws.
- Provider controls service descriptions, service areas, posted availability, quoted prices and fulfillment, subject to approved platform rules.
- No promise of lead volume, booked jobs, earnings, geographic exclusivity or continued availability.
- Responsibilities for service quality, safety, cancellations, injuries, property damage and insurance need negotiated, reviewed allocation before launch.

## 3. Payment and platform fees — proposed pilot
- During a limited founding-business early-access pilot, no recurring monthly membership is required.
- Proposed commission: **8% of successfully collected service payments**, deducted from the provider payout. **Do not add this as a customer surcharge.**
- Customer securely authorizes card storage and, after the provider marks a job complete, must **separately confirm completion and approve the displayed quoted charge** before RouteDrop attempts payment.
- A saved payment method is **not** permission to charge automatically merely because the provider marked the work complete.
- Define fees for reversals, refunds, disputes, failed payments, tax obligations, Connect platform costs and potential future subscription changes with counsel and Stripe review.

## 4. Customer experience and complaints
- Providers agree to accurate service descriptions, clear pricing and timely fulfillment.
- Define cancellation windows, provider/customer no-show obligations, refunds, disputes and responsible decision-makers. Existing sandbox UI includes a 24-hour cancellation concept and specific refund operations; that is **not approved live policy**.
- Define escalation, response time, supporting evidence, charge disputes and the provider's ability to appeal independent reviews.
- No unilateral re-charging after an unsuccessful payment without clearly valid customer authorization and Stripe-safe reconciliation.

## 5. Verification, insurance and restricted services
- Determine risk categories (transportation, childcare/care services, regulated trades and others) and appropriate exclusions or additional screening before rollout.
- Set required license and insurance documentation, verification steps, expiration reminders and revocation process by service category.
- A provider may not review its own credentials. Do not promise RouteDrop has certified a provider based solely on uploaded files.

## 6. Privacy and security
- Publish a reviewed provider-facing privacy notice including data categories, legal basis, processors, retention periods, access, incident handling and data-rights instructions.
- Accept sensitive real credentials **only after** secure malware scanning, encryption and key rotation, retention/deletion, independent assessment and appropriate account verification are operating.
- Synthetic preview uses fabricated accounts and sample documents only; no real provider IDs, insurance policies or banking data are authorized.
- Set confidentiality and permitted operational use of provider and customer information.

## 7. Suspension and ending participation
- Establish notice, appeals, dispute escalation, credential revocation and service shutdown procedures.
- Address the treatment of accepted bookings, outstanding service obligations, refunds and payment disputes on removal from the platform.

## 8. Legal provisions left for counsel
- Jurisdiction, governing law and consumer rights; representations/warranties; limitation of liability; indemnity; insurance; taxes; classification and independent-contractor implications; arbitration/class waiver if appropriate; intellectual property; notices; modifications; accessibility and age restrictions.
- Avoid presenting these provisions as approved without legal and insurance review.

## 9. Versioned consent and evidence
- Before production enrollment, publish an immutable approved version and date, obtain affirmative provider acceptance, record the user/business/version/text digest/timestamp, and provide a copy of accepted terms.
- A changed contract requires a new version and an appropriate re-acceptance mechanism before affected actions.
- The separately implemented **synthetic-preview acknowledgement** is not intended to count as acceptance of this agreement.
